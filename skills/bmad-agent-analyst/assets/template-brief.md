# {title}

## Table of Contents

## Executive summary
<!-- Distill what the other sections settled into two or three paragraphs of narrative, compelling enough to stand alone: what this is, the problem it solves, why it matters and why now. Draft it yourself, then ask the user whether a sponsor who reads nothing else would know what to do. Done when it says nothing the sections do not hold. -->

## Problem
<!-- Push hardest here, where briefs most often overclaim. Who feels the pain, how they cope today, and what the status quo costs them, with real scenarios, real frustrations and real consequences. Ask for the strongest evidence that someone actually has this problem; a problem the user assumes, rather than has seen, is said to be one. Done when the user can name someone who has it and what it costs them. -->

## Solution
<!-- What gets built, the form it takes (app, web tool, service), and how it solves the problem, as experience and outcome rather than implementation. Pull the user back from features and technology to what changes for the person in the problem. Done when each part of the solution answers a part of the problem. -->

## What makes it different
<!-- Push hard here too. Why this over the alternatives, including doing nothing; the real advantage, even when it is only speed or focus. Name the alternatives yourself when the user has not, and ask why a customer would switch. Done when the advantage would survive a customer saying "I already use something for that". -->

## Who it serves
<!-- Primary users, vivid but brief: who they are, what they need and what success looks like for them; secondary users when they matter. "Everyone" and "small businesses" are not users; ask for the first person who would use it. Done when the user could find that person. -->

## Success criteria
<!-- Measurable signs it is working, for users and for the business. Ask what number would make the user stop, not only what would make them celebrate. Done when each criterion could be checked. -->

## Scope
<!-- What the first version includes and what it leaves out: a tight boundary, not a feature list. Ask for the smallest version someone would pay for or switch to. When the list outruns what a first version needs, say so, suggest what could wait and why, and let the user decide. Done when the boundary is one the user would hold. -->

## The ask
<!-- include when: the brief pitches a sponsor. What the money or time buys, what is still unknown, the running cost, and when the sponsor can stop. -->

## Vision
<!-- optional. Where this goes in two or three years if it works, inspiring but grounded. -->
