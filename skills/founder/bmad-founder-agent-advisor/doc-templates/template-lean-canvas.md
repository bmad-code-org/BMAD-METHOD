# {title}

## Table of Contents

## Customer segments
<!-- One segment, and inside it the early adopters: the people who have the problem worst and are already trying to solve it. When the answer is "everyone" or a list, ask who would buy first and offer a second canvas for the rest. -->

## Problem
<!-- The top one to three problems this segment has, ranked, in their words. Ask how the founder knows: a problem heard from customers carries more weight than one inferred. -->

## Existing alternatives
<!-- What the segment does today about each problem, including doing nothing, hiring someone or a spreadsheet, and what it costs them. -->

## Unique value
<!-- One sentence: why this is different and worth attention, for this segment. When it lists features, ask what result the customer gets. -->

## Solution
<!-- The smallest thing that solves each problem listed, one line per problem. -->

## Channels
<!-- How the first customers will hear about it and buy it: the first channel, and why it reaches this segment. "Social media" and "word of mouth" are not channels until they say where and how. -->

## Revenue
<!-- Who pays, how (subscription, one-off, usage, commission), and the price, with any revenue to date and its date. -->

## Costs
<!-- What it costs each month to run the business and to reach customers, and what one customer costs to win and serve, each a figure or an open question. -->

## Key metric
<!-- The one number that shows the business is working at this stage, with its current value and date. Push past visits and signups to a number tied to use or money. -->

## Unfair advantage
<!-- What cannot easily be copied or bought: inside knowledge, a community, a network effect, exclusive access. "None yet" is fine; "first mover" and "passion" are not advantages. -->

## Riskiest assumption
<!-- The one belief on this canvas that would end the business if false, and the cheapest test that would show it. This is the box the canvas exists for: ask which box the founder is least sure of and which would hurt most if wrong; it is usually Customer segments or Problem, rarely Solution. When the test needs a pass mark, name `validation-experiment`. Done when the assumption is one sentence and its test has a date. -->
