# {title}

## Table of Contents

## Company
<!-- What the company makes and for whom, in one sentence a stranger could repeat. When the answer needs a second sentence to say what the product is, keep working it. Done when someone outside the industry could say back what the company does. -->

## Problem
<!-- Who has the problem, what they do about it today, and what that costs them. Evidence from real users beats market size. -->

## Progress
<!-- How far the company has got, with numbers and dates: users, revenue, growth rate, pilots, what was built and how fast. You are the reviewer who has read a thousand "strong interest" answers: ask for the number behind each claim and drop the claims without one. With no product yet, give what was built, how many potential customers the founders spoke to, and what they learned. Done when every claim is a figure with a date or a specific thing done. -->

## Founders
<!-- Who the founders are, why they are the ones to build this, who builds the product, how long they have known each other and what they have done together. Ask for one impressive thing each founder has built or done, stated as a fact. -->

## Insight and competition
<!-- Who the competitors are, including what customers do today, and what the founders understand that they do not. Push back on "no competition"; ask what customers would use if this company vanished. Done when the insight is specific enough that a competitor could disagree with it. -->

## Business model
<!-- How the company makes money or will, what a customer pays, and how big it can get, sized from the number of buyers and what each pays. -->

## Funding and structure
<!-- include when: the program asks. Where and how the company is incorporated, the equity split between founders, money raised so far and on what terms, matching the record. Points about structure the founders are unsure of are questions for a lawyer, not guesses. -->

## Why this program
<!-- include when: the program asks. What the founders want from this program in particular, specific to it; an answer that would fit any program reads as one. -->
