## Table of Contents

## Summary
<!-- The raise in a few lines: amount, instrument, what it buys, target close, stop date. Write it last. -->

## Why raise now
<!-- What the money is for, why outside money rather than revenue or no raise, and why now. Current cash and runway from the record. You are the advisor who has watched founders raise because they thought they had to: ask whether the company could reach its next milestone without this money, and what raising costs them in time and ownership. Done when the reason would hold if the founder had to say it to an investor who asked "why do you need this?" -->

## Milestones
<!-- What the company will have reached when the money runs low, each a number with a date, and why those milestones make the next round, or profitability, possible. Ask what the next investor will need to see; a milestone that does not answer that is not one the round should be sized for. Done when each milestone is a number, a date and the investor question it answers. -->

## Amount
<!-- How much, built from the monthly plan that reaches the milestones: people, costs, and a buffer for things taking longer. The months of runway it gives. When the figure comes back round or borrowed from another company's round, ask how it was built and rebuild it from the months. Never fill in a cost. Done when the amount, the monthly burn and the runway agree. -->

## Instrument
<!-- SAFE, convertible note, priced round, grant, revenue-based financing or a loan, and why it fits this stage and this amount. Ask where the company is incorporated; whether the instrument fits that jurisdiction and its investor tax schemes is a question for the lawyer. The terms expected, such as a valuation cap, discount or price, and the dilution the founder will accept. Name what the founder does not know about each term and write it as a question for a lawyer. -->

## Investors
<!-- The criteria first: stage, cheque size, sector, geography, whether they lead. Then the target list, grouped by priority, each with why they fit and the route in: a warm introduction by name, a program, or cold. Link each to their `people/` file. Ask how many conversations the founder expects per yes and whether the list is long enough for that; ask who could lead, because a round with no lead often does not close. Done when every named investor meets the criteria and has a route in. -->

## Materials
<!-- optional. What investors will ask for and its state: deck, financial model, data room, one-pager, product demo. Name the owner and the date for each that is not ready. -->

## Timeline
<!-- Preparation, first meetings, the weeks when most meetings happen, target close. Who runs the raise and how much of the founder's week it takes, and what in the company slows while it runs. diagram: gantt -->

## Stop date and plan B
<!-- The date and the signal that say this plan is not working, such as no lead committed by a date or fewer than a set number of second meetings, and what the founder does then: cut costs, a smaller or bridge round, revenue first, a different instrument. Founders resist writing this, so expect pushback. You are the advisor who would rather they decide this now, calmly, than in the last month of runway: ask what month the cash runs out, count back the time a fallback needs, and set the stop date there. Done when the stop date, the signal and the fallback are each written, and the fallback leaves enough runway to work. -->
