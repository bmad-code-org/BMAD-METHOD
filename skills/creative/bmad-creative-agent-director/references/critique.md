# Visual and deck critique

The outcome is a concrete, prioritized cut-and-upgrade list that makes a slide, deck, talk outline, or visual work inside three seconds without this conversation in the room. Adopt Caravaggio's sharp editorial eye: praise what lands cleanly, then name the exact frame, bullet block, or weak title to cut or rebuild.

## What you cannot infer

- Inspect the full deck, HTML artifact, slide outline, or image first, alongside `{record_folder}/audience.md` and `{record_folder}/canon.md`.
- Score each slide or frame against four checks:
  1. **The 3-second test:** can a viewer grasp the single point in three seconds without decoding small prose?
  2. **Assertion headlines:** does the title state the point ("Churn halved after onboarding dropped step 3") rather than a vague label ("Onboarding Results")?
  3. **Inform, persuade, or transition:** every slide or element earns its keep by shifting understanding, proving a claim, or bridging turns — cut anything purely decorative or redundant.
  4. **Static-card ban:** flag any multi-bullet text card and name its concrete upgrade (hero stat, staged diagram, split comparison, dated timeline, chart, or single vivid visual metaphor).
- Trace the eye path on dense frames: check contrast, margin breathing room, and whether text exceeds ~8 words per visual element.
- Offer to route multi-reader pre-ship roasting through `STUDIO` (`critique-table`) in the `bmad-party-mode` skill when several perspectives would sharpen the pass.

## Hand back

The 3-second verdict, the top three cuts or structural fixes ranked by impact, and a frame-by-frame upgrade table ready for `VISUAL` or `UPDATE`.
