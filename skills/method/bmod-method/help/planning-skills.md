# Planning skills in detail

Read this when the question is about Sofia (`bmad-agent-po`), John (`bmad-agent-pm`), `bmad-ux`, or Winston (`bmad-agent-architect`): what each gives, when to pick it, when not to, and what it writes.

**`bmad-agent-po`** — Sofia the Product Owner. Talk to her to write a ticket's requirements from any input and slice from them; the ticket file is the spec at its level.
- Gives: the ticket's Requirements and Constraints, one line per requirement with a stable `R<n>` id naming its source (`R1 (FR-2, FR-5)`) or, on an epic, its parent's id (`R1 (parent R3)`), plus its Assumption and Open question lines and companions beside the ticket. She adopts UX files and an architecture spine as References lines and absorbs a PRD or brief as a source. She then slices: an initiative into epics, an epic into entries whose `covers` cite the `R<n>` ids. She also updates and validates the requirements of an existing ticket. The user reaches her by name ("ask Sofia", "the product owner") or by saying what they want ("distill this PRD", "slice this initiative", "what's ready"); asked what she can do, she lists her capabilities.
- Pick when: the user has anything to distill, or an idea to work through with her; after any analysis or planning skill finishes, before architecture; when requirements change (she appends to the ticket's `.memlog.md`, re-derives the lines, and names the tickets that no longer match).
- Not when: the user wants to work a bare idea out first → Mary the Business Analyst for a brief, or John the Product Manager for a PRD when full requirements are needed. With sparse input she coaches too, walking the sections with the user.
- Writes: the ticket file, `<type>-<slug>.md` in its own `<type>-<slug>/` folder (the initiative's is `{output_folder}/{active_initiative}/`), with `.memlog.md` and companions beside it. Each container's breakdown sits in a `tickets/` folder inside it: `tickets.toml`, the epic folders under an initiative, the leaf files and plans under an epic.

**`bmad-agent-pm`** — John the Product Manager. Talk to him to coach a document that defines what gets built or how it ships out of the user: a program PRD (`prd-program`), a PRD for several capabilities (`prd`), a PRD for one epic (`prd-epic`), prototype plan, Shape Up pitch, requirements specification, or launch plan. A document that decides whether and why to go ahead (a brief, a PRFAQ, an opportunity assessment, a business case, a strategy narrative, a business requirements document) is Mary's (`bmad-agent-analyst`); the experiment card and the program charter belong to both. Each template names the agents that offer it by default, and either writes any document the user asks them for.
- Gives: the document that fits the work's altitude, sized to the stakes. A program gets a program PRD (`prd-program`) for outcomes, invariants, rollout strategy and initiative map, or a charter (`charter`) when governance is the question; an initiative a thin PRD with its epic map (`prd`), a pitch, requirements specification or launch plan; an epic, or any single capability, its own detailed PRD (`prd-epic`) or a pitch; a single story is a story ticket from Sofia (`bmad-agent-po`). He offers a prototype plan for a simple hobby project, or to prove out an idea or try the look and feel before committing. Once the user agrees, it can go to `bmad-build-auto`. An experiment card tests one hypothesis by measuring it. A business requirements document (`brd`) fits only when the business and the builder are separate parties, a sponsor signs a baseline, or a contract or regulator expects one; other product work inside one organization skips it. Mary (`bmad-agent-analyst`) writes it, and his PRD or requirements specification picks up its ids. Program and initiative documents state OKRs, invariants and a map (`INIT-01`.. or `E01`..) with stable ids for Sofia to lift; epic documents state testable requirements. He picks the template, says why, and names close alternatives. A run starts in the template's mode, step-by-step unless it sets one (the launch plan starts freeform); the user can switch to freeform or fast, and the pitch and experiment card keep their order. He also updates a document when the product moves, and validates one into review findings without changing it. The user reaches him by name ("ask John", "the PM", "the product manager") or by saying what they want ("create a PRD", "shape a pitch", "validate this PRD").
- Pick when: the user would rather work the idea out before it becomes the spec; a consumer or multi-stakeholder product; compliance, integration, or SLA concerns; a launch needs a document; an existing planning document needs editing or critique.
- Not when: scope is one or two stories → `bmad-build`. A lighter document will do → a brief from Mary the Business Analyst, then talk to Sofia the Product Owner to write the initiative from it. A brief is an optional input, never a prerequisite.
- Customizing: recommend `bmad-customize` for any change to his documents, such as a section the PRD must have or HTML by default. It extends his template in `_bmad/custom/facilitate-templates/`, so his updates still arrive, or sets doc standards, reviewers, output, style and the HTML view on `bmad-facilitate`, which runs his documents.
- Writes: `{output_folder}/{active_initiative}/<id>-<slug>/<id>-<slug>.md`, `<id>` the template (`prd`, `launch`, and so on); a pitch also writes its HTML view beside it. Run on an epic, the folder goes in the epic's folder, `<slug>` the epic's without `epic-`.

**`bmad-ux`** — captures how the product looks and how it works. It may lead, follow, or stand alone.
- Gives: `DESIGN.md` (visual tokens and rules) and `EXPERIENCE.md` (structure, states, interactions, accessibility, key flows), optionally mockups and wireframes. It captures the user's vision and never imposes one. A design-handoff mode builds a prompt for an external design tool.
- Pick when: the UI is a significant part of the work; the user wants to design first and derive requirements from the design; the user has design assets to fold in; design will happen in an outside tool but a contract is still needed.
- Not when: there is no meaningful UI.
- UX first: its files are good input to John the Product Manager for a PRD when requirements still need drawing out, to Mary the Business Analyst for a brief when a lighter write-up will do, or talk to Sofia the Product Owner to write the initiative from them when the design already says enough.
- In the ticket: both files are adopted as References lines. Change them with `bmad-ux` update, not through the ticket.
- Writes: `{output_folder}/{active_initiative}/ux-<slug>/`: `DESIGN.md`, `EXPERIENCE.md`, and `ux-<slug>.md` naming them. Run on an epic, `ux-<slug>/` goes in the epic's folder, `<slug>` the epic's without `epic-`.

**`bmad-agent-architect`** — Winston, the System Architect. Runs technical architecture, design, and readiness assessments via `bmad-facilitate`.
- For a user new to architecture: he coaches by default, recommends web-verified current starters when the stack is open, and lays out realistic trade-offs before the user chooses.
- Gives: core delivery spines (`arch-enterprise`, `arch-spine` with Coupling Radar & Contract Index, `arch-epic` with optional `contract.yaml`), proposals & contracts (`solution-design`, `tech-evaluation`, `api-design`, `decision-record`), codebase & AI readiness (`agent-readiness`, `arch-ai`), and risk & transition plans (`migration-plan`, `production-readiness`, `security-review`, `postmortem`). Every `AD-n` is checked against numeric `Passable` / `Good` `OKR-N` bounds and `INV-N` invariants.
- Pick when: two units built independently could choose incompatibly; a team needs an RFC/ARB sign-off, build-vs-buy bake-off, or public API contract; a repo has agent pattern drift or doc/comment bloat (`agent-readiness`); an AI/agent system needs guardrails and eval gates (`arch-ai`); or a migration or production launch needs safety gates.
- Not when: one bounded story fits existing patterns → `bmad-build`.
- Writes: `{output_folder}/{active_initiative}/<id>-<slug>/<id>-<slug>.md` (or `<epic folder>/arch-epic-<slug>/arch-epic-<slug>.md` at epic level).

## Slicing and tracking the work

Sofia the Product Owner plans and tracks work in one ticket tree. An initiative holds epics; each epic's `tickets.toml` holds ordered entries. Build an entry directly without making a story file. Standalone stories and bugs can be direct intent or backlog leaves. Plans own status and remain live after completion. Builds stop at `built`; the user or orchestrator marks `done`. See `help/ticketing-setup.md`.

She slices initiatives into epics, plans each epic's stories as entries, refines when needed, and manages the board and optional tracker publishing. A PRD or described intent is valid input. Requirements stay in the epic and entries cite them with `covers`. A file is needed for refinement or tracker publishing, not to start a build. Tracker stores are lightly tested.

Slicing an initiative agrees with the user what its epics are: the initiative's cohesive capabilities, each something a person could see working by itself. When the initiative's PRD carries an epic map, that is the split and Sofia only confirms it. Each new epic is written thin, with `refined: false`: Description, Outcome, Boundaries, `Done when` as far as known, and no Requirements yet.

Refining an epic is its own stage, run on request ("refine the booking epic") or before its stories are planned. Sofia reads the epic, the initiative and their companions. An epic with open product questions they do not answer (how it works for the user, its rules, what to measure) gets its own PRD: she hands to John for `prd-epic`. More design is `bmad-ux` or Winston (`bmad-agent-architect`, `arch-epic`) on the epic, and research is `bmad-deep-recon`. Each run lands in the epic's folder and becomes its companion. With nothing open, or on the user's word, she fills the epic in from the initiative's lines and what the PRD saved for it, and says why it needs no PRD. The epic then gets its Requirements and `refined: true`. An epic file with no `refined` key counts as refined.

When the work is more than one initiative's worth, Sofia proposes a parent initiative instead of deeper nesting. The parent is an ordinary initiative whose `tickets/tickets.toml` lists its child initiatives in build order, with the prerequisites between them; it holds no epics or stories. Each child is an ordinary initiative folder beside it, naming the parent's file in `sources:`. Set the active initiative to the child being worked, or to the parent to see the whole.
